# Card Selection

Consider the following when selecting individual cards.

## Rarity

The cube is mostly uncommons. Any card that has ever been printed at uncommon is allowed, even if it has also been printed at another rarity.

The rarity constraint is *not* strict. We may include commons here and there to patch a hole. Rares are likewise allowed, provided they match the vibe in terms of power level and complexity.

## Universes Beyond

Universes Beyond *are* allowed

## Hard Limitations

MTG is a big game with decades of expansions (and baggage). We are mindful about what we include and exclude. Right now that looks like:

- No searching libraries for anything other than a basic land. It slows down games too much. Also leads to repetitive play
- No double-faced cards. Bad for draft logistics
- No control effects. No effects that exile your card on my side of the table (eg [[Fiend Hunter]]). Easy to accidentally switch cards between decks
- No external minigames: monarch, dungeons, speed, etc
- No joke sets or gimmicks (stickers, contraptions, etc)
- No unmarked game state that persists past end of turn: soulbond, renown, etc. We might make an exception for exhaust, which is marked with +1/+1 counters
- No mutate
- No regenerate or protection
- No prepare or omens. Too easy to confuse with adventures
- No retrace. Repetitive gameplay
- Manifest and manifest dread are allowed. No other face-down cards on the battlefield

We may tweak this over time. But for the moment, these are hard rules. 

Open question: what about clues, treasures, etc? I'm inclined against them, but could be swayed

## Land Tutors

I'm pretty skeptical of any search/shuffle effects. They slow down the game a lot. My starting point is to exclude them entirely from the cube. 

Notably, this does *not* kill ramp and multicolor decks. We've still got a decent bench including [[Explore]], [[Clifftop Lookout]], [[Zimone's Experiment]], etc. 

That all said, I have observed that WOTC has put a *lot* of land tutors in recent sets -- landers, [[Evolving Wilds]], landcycling, etc. And these same sets seem to rate highly among players. Maybe mana screw is worse than shuffling. 

## Counters

Draft formats typically do not mix +1/+1 counters and -1/-1 counters in the same set. We abide by that rule. The cube includes +1/+1 counters and excludes any card that uses -1/-1 counters.

We could be open to relaxing this rule if there were a handful of cards that made -1/-1 counters attractive.

## Card Types

[[Disenchant]] effects are typically weak in draft. They're dead draws most of the time. Then, once in a while, you get blown out by an artifact or enchantment that you have no way to interact with. To me, that's bad tension. 

At present, we just skip them. The cube includes only creatures, sorceries, instants, and lands. No planeswalkers, no sagas, no auras, no mana rocks. Artifact creatures and enchantment creatures are allowed. 

I would not hate to change this, but we need to be honest about what that means. We can't just add a single mana rock or equipment. We would need: 
- Enough noncreature artifacts/enchantments for [[Disenchant]] effects to be plausible picks during the draft
- Enough [[Disenchant]] effects to ensure reasonable access

## Token Paperwork

In principle, any sort of creature token is allowed. However, let's do our best to limit the *total* number of creature tokens. 

I'd be skeptical of including (eg) [[Madame Hydra]] on her own, since we would have to keep track of 2/1 Villain tokens with menace. But if we can also make a case for [[Madame Masque]] or [[Hydra Troopers]] then that sort of token becomes more appealing.

This limitation does not apply to vanilla 1/1 tokens or a few other common ones, regardless of creature type. See `house-rules.md` for more information about token consolidation. 

## Counterplay

If it's possible to go wide, we need sweepers. If it's possible to go big, we need removal that can hit big creatures. If lands are important, we need (non-oppressive) ways to interact with them. If graveyard homework is a theme, we need a way to interact with that too.

We can also note: conditional interaction is more interesting than unconditional interaction. In a cube with a significant number of artifact creatures, [[Go for the Throat]] is just a more interesting card than [[Fell]]

## Power Creep

The power level of creatures in MTG has consistently increased over the years. Most uncommon creatures printed 5+ years ago are no longer suitable for the cube. They've just fallen too far behind compared to recent sets. 

This is not a hard rule. There are some old uncommons that were *extremely* overpowered at the time, which now are appropriately powerful.

The goal of the cube is for everything to be in the same ballpark in terms of power level. Some cards will always be better or worse. But we do not want any bombs that will run away with the game on their own, and we do not want any rarely-playable filler.

## Trinket Text

Sometimes a card is good overall, but also does stuff we don't care about. For example, [[Spikeshell Harrier]] is still good even if we ignore the part about reducing speed. 

This is fine. We can ignore the trinket text and irrelevant parasitic mechanics. In some cases we may even sharpie it out to avoid the confusion.
